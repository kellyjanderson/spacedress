# Code of Conduct

SpaceDress is a technical project. Strong criticism, failed experiments, and disagreement are expected. Personal hostility is not.

## Expected conduct

Contributors and maintainers are expected to:

- criticize ideas, designs, measurements, and code rather than people;
- distinguish verified facts from assumptions and hypotheses;
- correct errors directly and accept corrections without turning them into status contests;
- make room for different levels of macOS, Swift, accessibility, and reverse-engineering experience;
- avoid harassment, threats, discriminatory abuse, sexualized targeting, stalking, or deliberate intimidation;
- protect private information accidentally exposed in logs, screenshots, crash reports, or diagnostic bundles;
- disclose conflicts of interest that materially affect a technical or project decision.

## Technical disagreement

"This approach does not work" is acceptable when supported by evidence or a reproducible argument. "You do not understand this" is not a substitute for that evidence.

When facts are uncertain, say what would falsify the current hypothesis and design the next experiment.

## Scope

This code of conduct applies to project-controlled spaces including issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, releases, and project chat channels if any are created.

## Enforcement

Report conduct concerns to the project maintainer, [`@kellyjanderson`](https://github.com/kellyjanderson). Do not publish sensitive personal details in a public issue. GitHub's platform reporting tools may also be used where appropriate.

Maintainers may edit or remove content, close or lock discussions, reject contributions, temporarily restrict participation, or permanently ban participants when necessary to protect the project and its community.

Enforcement should be proportional, documented when privacy permits, and focused on behavior rather than viewpoint.
